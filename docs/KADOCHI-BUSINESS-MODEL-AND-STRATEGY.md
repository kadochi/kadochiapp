# Kadochi Business Model and Strategy

**Decision-ready operating strategy**  
**Market:** Tehran, Iran  
**Strategy date:** 4 August 2026  
**Operating horizon:** 12 months, with a three-year direction  
**Primary objective:** Preserve cash and prove repeatable unit economics while building a trusted occasion-and-gifting service  
**Capital constraint:** Approximately 20 million toman current cash; maximum 10–15 million toman optional monthly investment; effectively no working capital

---

## Executive decision

Kadochi should not try to win by becoming Iran's largest online gift catalog. At its current stage, that path would create working-capital risk, unreliable supplier dependencies, inconsistent preparation times, multiple delivery charges, and a weak reason for customers to return.

Kadochi should instead become Tehran's most dependable **occasion and gifting service**: a service that helps customers remember an important moment, decide what to send, prepare it beautifully, and deliver it reliably.

The capital constraint is now a governing fact, not a footnote. Approximately 100 million toman has already been spent and about 20 million remains in Kadochi's account. The previous spend is a **sunk cost**: it should be studied for lessons and reused through the infrastructure already built, but it must not justify further spending or create pressure to “win the money back” through risky inventory, advertising, hiring, or packaging commitments.

The next model must work with customer prepayment, supplier credit/consignment, existing infrastructure, founder labor, and low-cost modular presentation. The 10–15 million toman monthly amount is a ceiling, not a spending target. Unspent money stays in the business.

The brand promise is:

> **Never miss. Never guess. Reliably delight.**

This extends Kadochi's existing idea—“for seeing your smile”—into an operating position customers can understand and the team can measure.

The first-year model is deliberately narrow:

- Tehran-first delivery.
- B2C personal gifting as the core business.
- Prepaid, standardized B2B gifting pilots as a secondary cash-flow opportunity.
- Flowers remain an acquisition and occasion anchor, but no longer define the company.
- Six to ten base bundle recipes, presented as 12–16 customer-facing variants, create visible variety without inventory breadth.
- The default supply model is purchase-after-payment, supplier reservation, consignment, or made-to-order. Owned product inventory is prohibited until demand and cash gates are met.
- An existing Tehran hub is used only if it has no material incremental rent or fit-out cost; it consolidates, quality-checks, wraps, and dispatches multi-item gifts as one experience.
- Packaging uses two or three standard off-the-shelf formats with low-volume stickers, tags, cards, ribbon, or a reusable stamp. Custom-manufactured branded packaging is deferred.
- Instagram generates discovery, but Kadochi converts customers to its website, occasion calendar, phone-number-based account, reminders, and PWA so the customer relationship is owned.
- Growth spending is allowed only after product-level and order-level contribution margins are proven.

The revised first-year base case is not “become a large marketplace.” It is to reach approximately **50–60 successfully delivered orders per month** while maintaining a protected cash floor, at least a 20% variable contribution margin, and 95% on-time/in-full delivery. A 25–30-order downside case must remain survivable; a 90–100-order upside case must be funded primarily by order contribution rather than new owner cash.

### The seven decisions that govern the strategy

1. **Sell solved gifting decisions, not an endless catalog.** Hero bundles and guided selection are the core merchandising unit.
2. **Own the promise.** Kadochi—not the supplier or courier—is accountable to the customer for the final result.
3. **Buy after the sale.** Customer cash, consignment, supplier reservation, or short supplier settlement—not Kadochi's bank balance—funds product procurement. Only generic packaging consumables may be held initially.
4. **Consolidate before delivery.** Multi-supplier orders flow through the hub whenever practical; customers should not receive fragmented deliveries or pay invisible coordination costs.
5. **Use flowers as a component and entry point.** Flowers should increase the emotional value of a gift, not limit the assortment or positioning.
6. **Build the occasion relationship.** The calendar, reminder, recipient, wishlist, and order history create repeat demand that competitors cannot copy merely by listing more products.
7. **Protect the 12-million-toman floor.** New categories, paid acquisition, staff, product inventory, custom packaging, automation, and geography are unlocked only by explicit cash, economic, and service gates.

## 1. Market reality and its strategic implications

### 1.1 Economic conditions

Kadochi is operating in an unusually difficult demand environment. The IMF's July 2026 outlook for Iran projects a **5.4% contraction in real GDP** and **68.9% consumer-price inflation**. These numbers should not be treated as precise operating forecasts, but they correctly describe the strategic environment: purchasing power is under pressure, prices become stale quickly, imported goods are risky, and customers are more selective about non-essential spending. [Source: IMF Iran country page](https://www.imf.org/en/countries/irn)

The implications for Kadochi are direct:

- Do not compete primarily on luxury or imported brand names.
- Do not rely on a fixed annual price list.
- Do not finance broad inventory in anticipation of uncertain demand.
- Do not hide the full cost of fulfillment behind unsustainable delivery subsidies.
- Make each price tier feel complete and emotionally credible; an entry gift must not feel like a cheap fragment.
- Favor locally sourced, compact, durable, and replaceable products.
- Quote corporate work for short validity periods and collect deposits before procurement.
- Measure margin on every order after packaging, courier subsidy, payment cost, discount, and expected service recovery—not just product markup.

High inflation does not eliminate gifting. It changes the buying question from “What is the most impressive product?” to “How can I show care within a budget and be sure the result will be good?” That question is well aligned with Kadochi's gift finder, occasion features, curated bundles, packaging, and direct-to-recipient delivery.

### 1.2 E-commerce and channel behavior

Iran's 1403 e-commerce report indicates that 54.1% of surveyed online businesses used foreign social networks and messengers alongside their websites; summaries of the same report say approximately 94% were self-financed. This supports a channel strategy that combines social discovery with an owned transactional destination and restrained capital use. [Source: Iran 1403 e-commerce report summary](https://net-mag.ir/iran-e-commerce-report-1403-statistics-inmad/) and [report copy](https://ecomotive.ir/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/ec_report1403.pdf)

Recent internet restrictions make dependence on any one digital channel existentially risky. A May 2026 report citing the head of the Tehran Electronic Commerce Association said 80% of surveyed online businesses had lost more than half of their sales after the conflict began, and that Instagram, SMS, and messaging apps normally represented roughly three quarters of business-customer communication. The exact figures may vary by sample, but the operating lesson is clear: Kadochi needs an owned customer record, more than one communication path, and a reduced-capability continuity mode. [Source: IranWire](https://iranwire.com/en/news/152740-irans-internet-blackout-hits-online-sales-deepens-digital-divide/)

This means:

- Instagram is a demand-generation channel, not Kadochi's customer database.
- Every legitimate interaction should, with consent, move toward a phone-number-based account, website visit, occasion reminder, wishlist, or completed order.
- Kadochi should be able to accept, verify, fulfill, and communicate a limited set of orders when international channels are unavailable.
- Content production should be reusable across Instagram, the website, the magazine, SMS, push notifications, and domestic-access channels.

### 1.3 Competitive landscape

The market contains four relevant competitor types. Kadochi should learn from all four without copying their full models.

| Competitor type | Existing strength | Structural weakness or opening | Kadochi's response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flower and gift delivery networks | Same-day flower preparation, nationwide supplier coverage, established delivery expectations | Inconsistent city-by-city quality, substitution risk, and a transactional relationship centered on the current order | Win Tehran through controlled quality and build the next occasion through reminders and recipient memory |
| Broad online gift stores | Product variety, price comparison, SEO coverage, and add-on selling | Choice overload, inventory exposure, uneven readiness, and weak reliability across a very broad catalog | Offer fewer, fully solved bundles with explicit readiness and a single accountable promise |
| Intelligent gift-finder services | Personalized discovery by recipient, age, interests, and occasion | Advice may be disconnected from real inventory, delivery, and post-purchase execution | Connect discovery directly to fulfillable bundles, packaging, delivery, and retention |
| Corporate-gifting specialists | Customization, volume procurement, account selling, and large seasonal projects | Long sales cycles, credit risk, operational spikes, and a brand experience designed for organizations rather than individuals | Pilot prepaid standardized campaigns for smaller organizations without distracting from B2C |

Examples show how developed the promise already is. Wistore markets intelligent gift selection, B2C and B2B gifting, personalization, and a sub-three-hour Tehran service; TitiGift markets same-day flower/gift delivery, future-date scheduling, international payment options, and a distributed supplier network; GiftFinder focuses on recipient attributes and interests. [Wistore](https://wistore.ir/), [TitiGift FAQ](https://titigift.com/fa/faq), [GiftFinder](https://www.giftfinder.ir/)

Kadochi should not claim uniqueness merely because it has gift recommendations, flowers, or fast delivery. Its defensible difference is the combination of:

1. personal and public occasion memory;
2. guided choice from a deliberately controlled catalog;
3. one finished gift experience;
4. accountable Tehran fulfillment; and
5. a repeat relationship with both sender and, when consent exists, recipient.

## 2. Strategic position

### 2.1 Category definition

Kadochi is an **occasion and gifting service**.

It is not primarily:

- a flower shop;
- a product marketplace;
- a generic lifestyle retailer;
- a delivery company;
- an AI recommendation novelty; or
- a corporate merchandise agency.

The practical category promise is:

> Kadochi helps you remember the people and moments that matter, choose an appropriate gift within your budget, and have it prepared and delivered beautifully and reliably.

### 2.2 Positioning statement

For time-poor people in Tehran who want to make someone feel remembered, Kadochi is an accessible-premium occasion and gifting service that turns an uncertain, fragmented task into one guided and dependable experience. Unlike a broad gift marketplace or a supplier directory, Kadochi curates what can actually be prepared, combines it into complete gifts, owns quality and delivery, and helps the customer return for the next important moment.

### 2.3 Brand attributes

Kadochi should be perceived as:

- **Thoughtful:** recommendations fit the recipient and occasion.
- **Dependable:** the gift arrives within the promise and resembles what was purchased.
- **Warm:** communication feels human, not transactional.
- **Tasteful:** packaging and combinations are coherent rather than flashy for their own sake.
- **Accessible-premium:** visibly better than a commodity purchase, but available at more than one budget.
- **Calm:** the customer feels that Kadochi has removed work and uncertainty.

### 2.4 Strategic flywheel

1. Occasion content, Instagram, search, referrals, and shared wishlists create discovery.
2. The gift finder and curated collection reduce decision anxiety.
3. Explicit price and readiness bands build trust.
4. The hub and controlled supplier network create a successful delivery.
5. Packaging, the postcard, and recipient experience create a shareable moment.
6. The customer records the next occasion, leaves a review, or refers a recipient.
7. Reminders create lower-cost repeat demand.
8. Repeat demand reveals which bundles and suppliers deserve stock and promotion.
9. Better demand data improves availability, margin, and delivery reliability.

The flywheel only works if fulfillment is reliable. A late or visibly substituted gift is more damaging than an ordinary late parcel because the product represents a relationship and a specific moment.

## 3. Customer segments and jobs to be done

### 3.1 Primary segment: the time-poor personal sender

**Profile:** Approximately 25–45, smartphone-first, living in Tehran or sending to someone in Tehran, with enough disposable income for occasional discretionary purchases but strong price sensitivity.

**Situation:** A birthday, anniversary, thank-you, apology, romantic moment, family occasion, or “just because” gesture is approaching.

**Jobs:**

- Remind me before I am late.
- Help me choose without browsing for hours.
- Show options that fit both the person and my budget.
- Make an affordable gift feel complete.
- Deliver it when the recipient can receive it.
- Let me send it directly without ruining the surprise.
- Give me confidence that the result will resemble the page.

**Barriers:** Price uncertainty, choice overload, distrust of product images, late delivery, awkward substitutions, hidden delivery costs, and concern that an inexpensive gift will feel unimpressive.

**Kadochi response:** Guided bundles, budget bands, real fulfillment photography, visible delivery promises, recipient checkout, packaging, a personalized card, order status, and occasion reminders.

### 3.2 Secondary segment: the long-distance domestic sender

**Profile:** An Iranian outside Tehran—or temporarily away—sending to a recipient in Tehran.

**Jobs:**

- Be present when I cannot attend.
- Coordinate one complete gift from a distance.
- Know that the recipient actually received it.
- Avoid negotiating separately with a florist, cake shop, gift seller, and courier.

This segment values trust and status communication more than browsing depth. It can tolerate a small service premium when the final experience is controlled.

### 3.3 Recipient-to-sender loop

The recipient is not automatically a marketing lead. Kadochi must protect the sender's surprise and the recipient's privacy. With appropriate consent, however, the recipient can become:

- a reviewer;
- a referral source;
- a future sender;
- a wishlist creator; or
- a user who records their own important occasions.

The package should therefore contain a discreet Kadochi identity and a simple consent-based path such as “Someone thought of you—when you want to make someone else smile, Kadochi can help.” It should not include aggressive discount advertising that cheapens the gift.

### 3.4 B2B pilot segment

**Target organizations:** Tehran organizations with roughly 20–200 employees or a manageable customer-gifting need.

**Initial use cases:**

- employee birthdays;
- onboarding or welcome gifts;
- employee appreciation;
- customer thank-you gifts;
- Nowruz, Yalda, and selected organizational occasions; and
- small event or speaker gifts.

**Buyer:** Founder, HR lead, office manager, people-operations lead, or marketing manager.

**Jobs:**

- Do not make my team coordinate multiple suppliers.
- Give me presentable options at fixed budget levels.
- Collect addresses securely.
- Deliver on a schedule and report completion.
- Keep customization controlled and predictable.
- Give me one invoice and one accountable contact.

Kadochi should not pursue large tenders, complex branded merchandise, long payment terms, or nationwide simultaneous campaigns in year one.

### 3.5 Explicitly deprioritized segments

- Lowest-price shoppers whose main selection criterion is discount percentage.
- Customers seeking rare imported brands or authenticity-sensitive luxury goods.
- Nationwide same-day consumer delivery.
- Large organizations requiring extensive credit, tender participation, or bespoke procurement.
- Customers wanting Kadochi to source any arbitrary product on demand.
- High-return categories such as fashion sizing or technically complex electronics.

## 4. Value proposition and service design

### 4.1 The three customer promises

#### Promise 1: Never miss

Kadochi's occasion calendar, public occasion content, personal occasion entry, notifications, and PWA should make remembering a persistent service—not a campaign that appears only before Valentine's Day or Nowruz.

The commercial objective is to convert a transaction into a future permissioned reminder. The best post-purchase question is not merely “Would you review this?” It is also “Would you like Kadochi to remind you about this occasion next year?”

#### Promise 2: Never guess

The gift finder should lead to real, currently fulfillable choices using a small number of understandable inputs:

- recipient/relationship;
- occasion;
- budget band;
- urgency/readiness; and
- desired feeling or style.

Recommendations should favor three to six strong results, not another large search page. A manual concierge covers uncertainty while generating structured insight for future merchandising.

#### Promise 3: Reliably delight

Reliability means more than courier punctuality. A successfully delivered gift must:

- arrive within the promised window;
- contain the ordered or explicitly approved items;
- pass visual and packaging quality checks;
- preserve any food or flower quality requirement;
- include the correct postcard text;
- reach the intended recipient without exposing unnecessary sender information; and
- avoid a service complaint or refund within 48 hours.

This definition becomes Kadochi's north-star outcome: **successfully delivered gift orders**.

### 4.2 Customer journey

| Stage | Customer need | Kadochi behavior | Required evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remember | “What is coming up?” | Public/personal calendar, reminder, occasion content | Reminder delivered and opened |
| Decide | “What should I send?” | Gift finder, budget-led collections, concierge | Recommendation viewed, saved, or added |
| Trust | “Will it look right and arrive?” | Real images, clear contents, readiness label, delivery window, reviews | Product promise and price confirmed |
| Order | “Can I send it easily?” | Phone login, direct recipient details, saved address, packaging, postcard, ZarinPal payment | Paid order with complete fulfillment data |
| Prepare | “Is someone handling this?” | Confirmation, supplier acceptance, hub QC, status updates | All items present and quality checked |
| Deliver | “Did it arrive?” | Tracked courier handoff and recipient-safe communication | Delivered within slot |
| Retain | “How do I do this again?” | Review, occasion save, wishlist, repeat order, referral | Consent-based next action |

### 4.3 Service-recovery principle

When Kadochi causes or discovers a failure, communication must precede the promised window—not follow a customer complaint. The response order is:

1. explain the issue plainly;
2. offer the closest approved resolution and timing;
3. let the customer choose substitution, delay, store credit, or refund where applicable;
4. record the root cause; and
5. assign the financial cost to the order and supplier scorecard.

No material substitution should be made silently. Natural flower variation may be disclosed in advance, but container, color-family, stem-count range, gift-product, or value changes still require the policy stated on the product page and, when material, customer approval.

## 6. Offer and pricing architecture

### 6.1 Accessible-premium means three credible choices

Kadochi should not define “premium” only as expensive. It should mean that each offer feels intentional, is presented well, and has a dependable experience.

Because fixed toman bands become obsolete quickly, use a weekly **Kadochi Gift Index (KGI)**:

- Before sufficient Kadochi volume exists, KGI equals the median customer-visible price of 8–12 comparable, actively available core gift bundles from relevant Tehran competitors.
- After Kadochi has at least 30 completed gift orders in a rolling 30-day period, KGI becomes the median merchandise value of its ten highest-volume non-outlier completed bundles, excluding delivery.
- The merchandising owner updates the index weekly and records the source values.

Recommended price architecture:

| Band | Relative range | Customer role | Merchandising rule |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Entry | 0.55–0.80 × KGI | “I want to show care without overspending” | Must look complete; typically one anchor item plus presentation/card |
| Core | 0.90–1.20 × KGI | Default recommendation | Best balance of emotional impact, margin, and broad relevance |
| Premium | 1.50–2.25 × KGI | Important moments and higher willingness to spend | Adds quality, personalization, size, or experience—not random item count |

Each collection page and concierge response should present a maximum of one or two strong options per band before showing more products.

### 6.2 Hero bundles

Launch with **six to ten operational base recipes**, presented as **12–16 customer-facing variants** through flower size, message, or one controlled add-on. This produces useful choice without funding separate products, photography, packaging, and backup stock for 16 materially different bundles. Expand only when a new bundle answers a repeated customer request and can be purchased after the order.

#### Wedge A: celebration foods

- Locally made chocolates or confectionery.
- Tea/coffee, nuts, dried fruit, or small shelf-stable treats.
- Cakes only where the bakery can meet an explicit lead time and handling standard.
- Clear ingredient/allergen and storage information.

#### Wedge B: sentimental and personalized gifts

- Printed photo/card products.
- Small engravable items from a reliable local producer.
- Journals, message books, or keepsake cards.
- Personalized items remain scheduled, not same-day, until the supplier proves otherwise.

#### Wedge C: small local lifestyle and self-care

- Candles, small home/desk objects, locally made care products, mugs, or practical accessories.
- Products must have clear provenance and low authenticity risk.
- Avoid regulated or claim-heavy products unless documentation and handling are robust.

#### Wedge D: universal add-ons

- Premium postcard and envelope options.
- Balloons, candles, ribbons, small plush items, decorative picks, or small plants where handling is reliable.
- These should attach to several anchor categories. Initially they are supplier-reserved or purchased after payment; only generic cards, ribbon, filler, and other packaging consumables are stocked.

Flowers can appear as an anchor, a component, or an add-on across the wedges. They should not be the only visual or emotional center of the assortment.

### 6.3 Example bundle logic

Bundle names should communicate a feeling or use case, not just list contents. Examples include:

- A Little Celebration — confectionery, candle, postcard, compact flower accent.
- Thinking of You — tea/coffee, keepsake card, small local object.
- Birthday Ready — small cake or treat, candles, balloon option, flowers, postcard.
- Quiet Appreciation — local food or desk gift, tasteful wrap, thank-you card.
- Our Memory — printed photo/keepsake, flower accent, personalized message.
- Welcome to the Team — practical desk item, local treat, branded note for B2B.

Names and final contents should be validated in Persian with customers; the examples above define the merchandising role, not required public copy.

### 6.4 Bundle construction rules

Every hero bundle must have:

- one emotional or practical anchor;
- a clear reason the items belong together;
- one lead external supplier plus Kadochi's generic card/wrap during the first 90 days; a second supplier only for scheduled orders that preserve at least 25% contribution;
- one accountable preparation promise;
- a final packed size and handling method;
- a real photographed reference unit;
- a standard postcard option;
- entry/core/premium differentiation based on quality or meaning, not filler;
- a target product gross margin of at least 35%; and
- a backup plan for any component whose absence would break the bundle.

No launch bundle may require Kadochi to buy finished-goods stock in advance. A sample unit can be borrowed, returned, supplier-funded, or purchased from the capped sampling budget; once photographed, it should be sold as a disclosed sample/ready unit where quality permits rather than retained as dead stock.

If a bundle repeatedly requires more than one emergency courier, it should be redesigned, moved to scheduled delivery, or discontinued.

### 6.5 Capital-light packaging system

Custom-manufactured branded boxes, bags, paper, or inserts are out of scope. A 200–300 million toman packaging commitment is 10–15 times current cash and cannot be justified at this stage.

Kadochi should create a recognizable unboxing system from modular, readily available materials:

- no more than two box sizes and one bag/wrap format at launch;
- neutral kraft, white, or another consistently available base material;
- one brand-color ribbon or tissue/filler combination;
- a low-volume digitally printed sticker, tag, or small reusable stamp;
- a standard postcard and envelope with print-on-demand personalization; and
- one assembly guide showing the placement and appearance of every element.

The initial modular-packaging purchase is capped at **1 million toman** and should cover only approximately ten orders. Replenishment happens from paid-order contribution and is included in each order's variable cost. Packaging cost should normally remain at or below 5% of merchandise revenue; higher-cost presentation must be sold as a paid upgrade while preserving the contribution floor.

Custom packaging can be reconsidered only when all of the following are true:

- six consecutive months of positive operating cash flow;
- the purchase leaves at least six months of fixed operating costs and all customer liabilities in cash;
- a per-order payback model shows recovery in no more than 12 months;
- modular-packaging tests show a measurable conversion, repeat, damage, or labor benefit; and
- the commitment is no more than 15% of unrestricted cash after payment.

The operating forecast assumes **no custom-manufactured packaging in years one or two**. The gate above—not a date—can change that decision.

### 6.6 Promotion policy

- Do not train customers to wait for permanent percentage discounts.
- Prefer added value—postcard upgrade, small add-on, bundle advantage, or referral credit—over headline price cuts.
- A sitewide discount is prohibited unless the finance owner models contribution margin after the discount and sets a hard order/cost cap.
- Free delivery is prohibited during the first 90 days. Later it may be tested only above a merchandise threshold of at least 1.4 times current AOV and only when expected contribution margin remains at least 20%.
- Supplier-funded promotions must still meet Kadochi quality and availability rules.
- Expiring inventory can be moved into an honest limited bundle; it must not be hidden in a premium gift.

## 7. Controlled catalog strategy

### 7.1 Three-tier supply model

#### Tier 1 — Order Funded: purchase after customer payment

**Purpose:** Keep product working-capital need at zero while Kadochi learns real demand.

**Suitable products:** Flowers, cakes, confectionery, local lifestyle products, personalized goods, and bundle components whose suppliers can confirm and prepare after an order.

**Rules:**

- Kadochi checks supplier availability and current cost before promotion and again immediately after payment.
- Product procurement begins only after the gateway has confirmed customer payment and the order data is complete. If bank settlement is still pending, only the ring-fenced revolving order float may bridge the timing gap.
- Supplier cost, inbound collection, outbound delivery, packaging, payment fees, and recovery reserve must all fit inside the price floor.
- Orders default to next-day or scheduled delivery when same-day consolidation would require an uneconomic emergency collection.
- Kadochi does not accept supplier minimum-order quantities or advance inventory purchases for an unproven SKU.
- If confirmation risk is high, the product is “request confirmation” or removed rather than funded speculatively.

#### Tier 2 — Partner Reserved: reservation, consignment, or weekly settlement

**Purpose:** Offer variety without purchasing stock while retaining a dependable fulfillment promise.

**Suitable products:** Local lifestyle goods, confectionery, small plants, selected giftware, or components a supplier can reserve and update reliably.

**Rules:**

- Supplier confirms current stock and locks price on order acceptance.
- Kadochi runs scheduled collection routes, asks suppliers to drop at the hub, or groups orders rather than sending an individual courier for every component.
- Consignment terms state ownership, damage responsibility, stock counts, and settlement schedule.
- Weekly settlement is preferred after successful delivery; second preference is payment on collection from already-cleared customer funds.
- Suppliers should provide photo samples on loan or allow Kadochi to photograph at their location before Kadochi purchases samples.

#### Tier 3 — Kadochi Ready: earned micro-inventory

**Purpose:** Stock only a proven component when doing so measurably reduces failures or collection cost and the purchase is funded from accumulated contribution—not the protected cash floor or owner injection.

**Rules:**

- No finished-goods inventory is purchased during the first 90 days. Generic packaging consumables are not considered finished goods.
- A candidate needs at least ten completed orders or a 35%+ attachment rate across 30 eligible orders, with no unresolved quality failure.
- No more than 3–5 finished-goods SKUs may be active in Tier 3 during year one.
- The purchase is the lower of seven proven demand days or three units per SKU.
- Total finished-goods inventory cost may not exceed 10% of unrestricted cash above the protected floor.
- No stocked SKU may exceed 14 forecast days of demand; the earlier 21-day ceiling is replaced by this stricter cash-constrained limit.
- Products with no sale in 21 days are frozen from replenishment; at 30 days they are returned, bundled honestly, or liquidated.
- Same-day status remains earned at SKU/supplier level and can be removed immediately.

Made-to-order products continue to require a documented lead time, cutoff, variation policy, and capacity limit. Seasonal capacity is reconfirmed before any campaign goes live.

### 7.2 Category admission scorecard

An item must pass all hard gates and score at least 70/100.

#### Hard gates

- Legal and safe to sell, store, and transport.
- Authenticity/provenance can be supported.
- Expected product gross margin is at least 35%.
- Supplier accepts a written availability, price, and preparation process.
- Procurement can be funded by customer prepayment, consignment, or approved supplier settlement; an unproven SKU cannot require Kadochi inventory cash.
- Product fits approved packaging/handling or has a priced exception.
- Customer-visible lead time can be stated honestly.
- A quality reference and customer-facing contents description exist.

#### Weighted score

| Dimension | Weight | What earns a high score |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer relevance | 20 | Fits several common recipient/occasion jobs and has observed demand |
| Margin quality | 20 | Strong gross margin without unrealistic list pricing or discounts |
| Supply reliability | 20 | Accurate stock/price, proven SLA, and credible backup source |
| Operational simplicity | 15 | Compact, durable, easy to QC and package, low return risk |
| Cash efficiency | 15 | Post-order purchase, consignment, short lead time, or rapid turns |
| Bundle compatibility | 10 | Combines naturally with several existing anchors and price bands |

### 7.3 SKU lifecycle

1. **Candidate:** Supplier and economics reviewed; not customer-visible.
2. **Test:** Limited listing or bundle placement for 30 days/ten orders.
3. **Proven:** Meets margin, attachment, quality, and fulfillment gates.
4. **Scale:** Receives content, stock, better terms, or more prominent placement.
5. **Seasonal:** Activated only around a defined occasion with capacity confirmed.
6. **Probation:** Quality, margin, or fulfillment below threshold; promotion stopped.
7. **Delisted:** Removed until the underlying issue is demonstrably corrected.

Catalog review happens weekly. “New” is not a reason to keep a SKU; only customer usefulness, economics, and fulfillment quality are.

### 7.4 Categories to avoid initially

- Imported branded products with currency and authenticity risk.
- Electronics requiring warranty, technical support, or model compatibility.
- Clothing, footwear, or other size-dependent products.
- High-value jewelry or cash-like goods.
- Cosmetics or fragrances without dependable authenticity and regulatory provenance.
- Large, highly fragile, temperature-sensitive, or courier-incompatible products.
- Products requiring many customer approvals or lengthy custom design.
- Slow-moving décor bought solely because it photographs well.

## 8. Supplier and fulfillment operating model

### 8.1 The operating principle

The customer purchases from Kadochi, not from a hidden collection of suppliers. Kadochi therefore owns availability communication, QC, packaging, delivery coordination, and service recovery.

The default commercial and physical flow is:

> Supplier price/availability pre-check → customer payment → final supplier acceptance and procurement → scheduled collection or supplier drop-off → Kadochi hub consolidation → item QC → modular packaging/card check → courier dispatch → delivery confirmation → contribution released after the complaint window

Supplier-direct delivery is allowed only for products that cannot reasonably pass through the hub, such as large flower arrangements, and only when the supplier meets Kadochi presentation, communication, and proof standards.

### 8.2 Delivery promise classes

Each customer-visible SKU and bundle receives one of four labels:

| Promise | Eligibility | Initial operating rule |
|---|---|---|
| Ready today | Flowers/supplier-direct products or a fully confirmed basket that needs no speculative purchase or emergency multi-supplier collection | Order by 13:00 for the 16:00–19:00 window; remove the promise whenever courier or supplier economics fail |
| Next day | Default for non-flower bundles; all components can be purchased from cleared customer funds and consolidated by the next operating day | Order by 18:00; three-hour delivery window selected at checkout |
| Scheduled | Cake, personalization, special flowers, B2B, or constrained production | Explicit earliest date and lead time; capacity checked before acceptance |
| Request confirmation | Rare exception for high-variation products | Not promoted; customer is contacted before final commitment/payment where technically possible |

The current site can support delivery slots and same-day eligibility. The merchandising and operational labels must be aligned with those capabilities so the site never offers a slot that the supply chain cannot meet. Under the cash-constrained model, **next day is the default non-flower promise**. Same-day variety should not be purchased with Kadochi's cash or assembled through two uneconomic collection trips merely to create a marketing claim.

For mixed baskets:

- The whole basket inherits the slowest component's promise.
- Split delivery is not the default.
- If a customer requests split delivery, show the additional charge and obtain explicit confirmation.
- Kadochi may propose replacing the slow component with an approved ready alternative, but cannot substitute without consent.

### 8.3 Daily order-control process

For every paid order:

1. **Within 10 minutes:** Order data is checked for recipient, address, slot, product readiness, packaging, and postcard completeness.
2. **Within 15 minutes:** Same-day suppliers accept and lock price; next-day/scheduled suppliers accept within 30 minutes during operating hours.
3. **Within 20 minutes of a failed confirmation:** Customer support contacts the buyer with alternatives or refund/timing choices.
4. **At least two hours before dispatch:** All consolidated components arrive at the hub.
5. **At least one hour before dispatch:** QC, packaging, postcard, label, and recipient-safe communication are complete.
6. **At courier handoff:** Package condition and courier identity/time are recorded.
7. **At delivery:** Delivery time and recipient result are recorded; failed attendance triggers the published re-delivery policy.
8. **Within 24–48 hours:** High-value or first-time orders receive a light satisfaction/review request.

At under 30 orders per month, this can run through a disciplined shared order-control sheet plus WooCommerce. Automation should follow repeated failure points, not precede operational learning.

### 8.4 Hub design

The small hub is used only while its incremental cash cost is negligible. Kadochi should not sign a new lease, renovate, buy shelving, or purchase specialized equipment in year one. Existing tables, shelves, labeled reusable containers, and phone-camera lighting are sufficient. Initial hub organization and basic tools are capped at **0.5 million toman**; if incremental hub cost later exceeds 2 million toman per month before the hub is supported by order contribution, use supplier drop-off, short-duration borrowed space, or a simpler scheduled assembly model.

Within that constraint, the hub needs clearly separated zones:

- inbound/awaiting QC;
- approved inventory;
- food-safe or storage-specific area;
- packaging materials;
- order assembly;
- completed/awaiting courier;
- exception/return quarantine; and
- a simple photography/QC station with consistent light and background.

Each order receives a checklist with order ID, contents, quantities, packaging type, postcard confirmation, recipient, delivery slot, special handling, checker initials, and handoff time.

The objective is not warehouse sophistication. It is preventing swapped cards, missing components, damaged presentation, and untraceable exceptions.

### 8.5 Supplier agreement minimum terms

Every active supplier agreement should cover:

- legal/business identity and settlement information;
- approved SKUs and specifications;
- availability-update method and frequency;
- price list, validity period, and price-lock moment;
- order acceptance channel and response SLA;
- preparation lead time, daily capacity, and seasonal exceptions;
- packaging/transport responsibility before Kadochi QC;
- acceptable natural variation and prohibited substitutions;
- reference imagery and quality standard;
- defect, cancellation, and late-delivery responsibility;
- rework/refund/service-recovery contribution;
- consignment ownership and stock-count rules where applicable;
- no mandatory inventory purchase or minimum-order quantity during validation;
- sample-loan, on-site photography, or paid-sample terms;
- payment after customer funds clear, with weekly post-delivery settlement preferred;
- weekly settlement and dispute process;
- customer-data confidentiality; and
- termination/probation conditions.

Critical components require at least one approved backup supplier. “Critical” means their failure would cancel a hero bundle or a major campaign.

During validation, add no more than one new active supplier per month. New supplier count is not progress unless the supplier fills a repeated demand gap or improves margin/reliability.

### 8.6 Supplier scorecard

Score suppliers monthly, and weekly during high-volume occasions.

| Dimension | Weight | Measurement |
|---|---:|---|
| On-time/in-full preparation | 35 | Accepted lines ready to spec by deadline |
| Quality conformity | 25 | Lines passing first QC without rework or complaint |
| Stock and price accuracy | 15 | Orders matching last confirmed availability/price |
| Confirmation speed | 10 | Accepted/rejected inside response SLA |
| Issue resolution | 10 | Speed and fairness of rework, replacement, or credit |
| Economics | 5 | Margin stability and settlement discipline |

Supplier tiers:

- **90–100 Preferred:** Eligible for hero bundles, campaigns, and volume discussions.
- **80–89 Approved:** Normal active supplier.
- **70–79 Probation:** Promotion paused; corrective action required within 30 days.
- **Below 70 Suspended:** New orders stopped until requalification.

No supplier is protected by personal relationship or past performance when current customer outcomes fall below the threshold.

### 8.7 Replenishment and stock control

There is no finished-goods replenishment during the first 90 days. For generic packaging consumables and any product inventory later earned through the Tier 3 gate:

> **Reorder point = average daily demand × supplier lead time + safety stock**

At low volume, use whole-unit judgment but retain the rule:

- Safety stock is normally one unit and never more than three units.
- Total finished-goods stock must remain below 14 expected days of proven demand and below the unrestricted-cash cap.
- Seasonal purchases use the downside forecast unless customers or B2B buyers have prepaid.
- Packaging consumables can carry up to 30 days of coverage only when they are generic, reusable across categories, and purchased from paid-order contribution.
- A weekly stock count reconciles system quantity, physical quantity, reserved orders, damage, and consignment ownership.

### 8.8 Fulfillment targets

- Successfully delivered/on-time-and-in-full: **at least 95%**.
- Supplier-caused cancellation: **below 2%**.
- Material substitution: **below 5%**, with 100% approval where required.
- Damage, quality refund, or presentation complaint: **below 3%**.
- Wrong/missing postcard or recipient-data error: **below 0.5%**.
- Same-day supplier acceptance inside 15 minutes: **at least 95% during operating hours**.

When any measure breaches its threshold for two consecutive weeks, promotion of affected SKUs/suppliers stops until a documented correction is tested.

## 9. Capital preservation, unit economics, and pricing control

### 9.1 Starting capital position

The operating model begins with:

- approximately **20 million toman gross cash** in Kadochi's account;
- approximately **100 million toman already spent**, treated as sunk cost;
- no meaningful product working capital; and
- a possible **10–15 million toman monthly owner investment**, treated as a maximum ceiling that may not arrive and should not be pre-committed.

This plan assumes there are no undisclosed unpaid taxes, salaries, rent, supplier balances, refunds, or other liabilities. If any exist, they are deducted before cash is considered available.

Customer receipts for paid but undelivered orders are restricted order funds, not operating cash. They become available only after the order has been delivered, supplier/courier obligations are covered, and the immediate 48-hour complaint window has passed.

### 9.2 Immediate allocation of the current 20 million toman

In the cash tables, **M means million toman**.

| Cash purpose | Ceiling | Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Hard survival reserve | 10M | Never used for ads, product inventory, development, routine discounts, or packaging upgrades |
| Refund/service-recovery reserve | 2M | Used only for refunds, urgent remake, or delivery recovery; together with survival reserve creates the 12M hard floor |
| Revolving order float | 3M | Bridges payment-gateway settlement timing after customer payment is verified; cannot fund speculative stock |
| Generic modular packaging | 1M | Two standard formats and approximately ten orders of neutral consumables; no custom-manufactured packaging |
| Catalog/content samples | 1M | Supplier loans/on-site photography first; purchased samples must be sold, consumed, or reused |
| Essential continuity costs | 1.5M | Only unavoidable hosting, communications, domain, or basic operating costs not already covered |
| Hub/supplier/admin setup | 0.5M | Existing assets first; low-cost labels, forms, or required administration only |
| Gated demand-test reserve | 1M | Locked during days 0–30; released only after order-economics gates pass |
| **Total** | **20M** | Ceilings are not spending targets; unused money remains cash |

The **12 million toman survival-and-recovery floor is the minimum** and exists in addition to full coverage of paid undelivered order commitments. If one month of unavoidable fixed cash costs plus the 2M recovery reserve exceeds 12M, that higher number becomes the protected floor.

Use this formula every day:

> **Unrestricted cash = bank cash − paid-undelivered order liabilities − other committed liabilities**

> **Protected floor = max(12M, one month unavoidable fixed cash cost + 2M recovery reserve)**

> **Spendable cash = max(0, unrestricted cash − protected floor)**

Maintain a 13-week forecast with one column per week and these rows: opening unrestricted cash, owner cash actually received, restricted customer receipts, contribution released after delivery, unavoidable fixed payments, approved discretionary payments, refunds/recovery, closing unrestricted cash, and lowest cash status. Expected-but-unreceived owner funding appears only in a separate scenario row and cannot support a commitment.

### 9.3 Monthly 10–15 million toman investment policy

Release new owner cash weekly, not as an invitation to spend the full monthly amount. The default use is to extend runway.

| Monthly use | If 10M is available | If 15M is available | Release rule |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Added reserve/runway | 6M | 8M | Automatic destination for every unused category |
| Essential platform/communications | 1.5M | 2M | Actual unavoidable cost only; existing infrastructure first |
| Founder-led organic content/merchandising | 1M | 1.5M | Batch-produced with supplier/UGC assets; no agency retainer |
| Retention, SMS, interviews, referrals | 0.5M | 1M | Consent-based and tied to a measurable cohort/test |
| Packaging, QC, and supplier samples | 0.5M | 1M | Replenishment follows paid orders; supplier loans preferred |
| Conditional acquisition tests | 0.5M | 1.5M | Zero in days 0–30; unreleased amount returns to reserve |
| **Total** | **10M** | **15M** | No category may borrow from the reserve without a cash-gate decision |

Paid-acquisition release schedule:

- Days 0–30: zero paid acquisition.
- Days 31–60: maximum 0.75M, only if the latest ten delivered orders have complete cost data and positive aggregate contribution.
- Days 61–90: maximum 1M, only if the first test is contribution-positive after attributed acquisition cost.
- Thereafter: maximum is the lower of 1.5M or 10% of the prior month's pre-acquisition contribution until two consecutive cohorts are first-order contribution-positive.

Avoid fixed influencer fees. Prefer founder-led content, supplier-provided assets, UGC with permission, affiliates, and referral rewards payable only after a successfully delivered, contribution-positive order.

No monthly investment may fund finished-goods inventory, custom packaging, a new lease, an agency retainer, a permanent hire, or nonessential software.

### 9.4 Cash stop-losses

| Status | Trigger | Automatic response |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Unrestricted cash ≥protected floor and customer obligations fully covered | Operate within released weekly budget |
| Amber | Unrestricted cash <protected floor | Stop paid promotion, samples, development, delivery subsidies, inventory, and nonessential packaging purchases |
| Red | Unrestricted cash <8M | Offer only prepaid, single-supplier, contribution-positive products; pause multi-supplier bundles and customized B2B |
| Critical | Unrestricted cash <5M, or cash no longer covers paid undelivered orders | Stop accepting orders requiring any Kadochi advance; resolve customer obligations first |

Additional stop-loss rules:

- Never commit spending based on a future monthly injection.
- Do not offer cash on delivery, post-delivery consumer credit, or internal installment financing.
- No single Kadochi-funded order advance may exceed the lower of 2M or 10% of unrestricted cash; the normal target is zero.
- Freeze discretionary spending after any rolling 30-day period with negative aggregate variable contribution.
- Pause a bundle after two margin failures or two lateness/substitution incidents in its latest ten orders.
- Stop a paid test at its approved cap, at zero orders after 1M spend, or when observed CAC exceeds available first-order contribution.
- Do not add a fixed-cost role because of one occasion spike. A new role requires three consecutive months in which contribution covers at least 1.5 times its fully loaded cost, plus unrestricted cash for three additional months.
- If owner funding exceeds 15M in each of two consecutive months without improving successfully delivered orders, contribution, or repeat behavior, return to the maintenance catalog and reset the plan.

### 9.5 Definitions

Kadochi should calculate economics at order and SKU/bundle level.

**Merchandise revenue**  
Product and bundle amount collected, excluding delivery and taxes where applicable, after product discounts and product refunds.

**Landed product cost**  
Supplier purchase cost plus inbound collection specifically attributable to the item, spoilage allowance, and unavoidable preparation charges.

**Product gross profit**  
Merchandise revenue minus landed product cost.

**Product gross margin**  
Product gross profit divided by merchandise revenue.

**Net order revenue**  
All customer revenue retained—merchandise, packaging, personalization, and delivery—after discounts, refunds, and taxes/pass-through amounts.

**Variable order cost**  
Landed product cost, final packaging consumables, payment fee, outbound courier cost, delivery subsidy, per-order fulfillment labor, affiliate/paid acquisition directly attached to the order, and expected refund/service-recovery cost.

**Variable contribution**  
Net order revenue minus variable order cost.

**Variable contribution margin**  
Variable contribution divided by net order revenue.

Fixed hub rent, core salaries, technology, and general content costs are not variable contribution costs; they are paid from total monthly contribution.

### 9.6 Financial guardrails

- New non-flower SKU product gross margin: **at least 35%**, with a 40% launch target.
- Blended monthly product gross margin: **at least 38%**.
- Pre-acquisition variable contribution margin after delivery and service recovery: **at least 20%**, with a 22% launch target.
- Multi-supplier bundle variable contribution margin: **at least 25%** because coordination and failure risk are higher.
- Expected recovery allowance: the higher of actual trailing experience or 3% of net order revenue.
- Absolute contribution per order: at least the greater of one standard same-zone Tehran courier fee or unavoidable monthly cash cost divided by the conservative monthly fulfilled-order target.
- Paid acquisition does not scale until two consecutive monthly cohorts have non-negative first-order contribution after attributed acquisition cost.
- When cohort data is mature enough, target 90-day gross-contribution LTV/CAC of at least **3.0**.
- Finished-goods inventory: zero in the first 90 days; thereafter no more than 14 forecast days, 3–5 proven SKUs, and 10% of unrestricted cash above the protected floor.
- Delivery is charged at actual cost during the first 90 days. Any later subsidy must preserve the 20% contribution floor.
- Company-funded discounts are capped at 3% of monthly net revenue and may not break the order contribution floor.
- B2B variable contribution margin: **at least 20%** after customization, project coordination, and delivery.
- B2B prepayment: 100% preferred. If split, the deposit is the higher of 80% of invoice value or 110% of supplier, packaging, delivery, cancellation, and tax commitments; the balance is due before dispatch.
- B2B quote validity: normally **24 hours** in volatile categories.

Monthly break-even is:

> **Unavoidable monthly cash cost ÷ average contribution per successfully delivered order**

Order volume without sufficient absolute contribution is not progress toward sustainability.

### 9.7 Price-floor formula

For an offer with percentage-based payment cost and a fixed variable cost:

> **Minimum price = (landed product cost + fixed variable costs + intended delivery subsidy) ÷ (1 − payment-fee rate − target contribution-margin rate)**

If the market will not support that price, Kadochi must change the contents, supplier, packaging, delivery promise, or promotion. It should not knowingly sell an economically broken bundle in the hope that future scale will repair it.

### 9.8 Inflation and repricing rules

- High-volatility suppliers submit a daily price/availability update before opening.
- Other suppliers update at least weekly and immediately when cost changes exceed 5%.
- Customer price is locked after payment and supplier acceptance.
- If supplier cost changes between payment and acceptance, Kadochi honors the order when the service-recovery cost is tolerable; repeated cases reduce the supplier's score.
- Merchandising reviews retail prices daily for flowers, imported inputs, and affected foods; weekly for stable local goods.
- Corporate quotes name the validity period and quantity assumptions.
- Discounts are calculated from the current real selling price, never from an inflated fictional list price.

### 9.9 Delivery economics

Delivery should be transparent and passed through during the first 90 days. A later subsidy is a measured acquisition or merchandising test, not an untracked default.

Track:

- courier cost per order;
- customer delivery revenue;
- net delivery subsidy;
- number of supplier collection trips;
- failed delivery/re-delivery cost;
- orders per pickup route; and
- delivery cost as a percentage of AOV.

The hub model succeeds when multiple components are consolidated through planned routes. If supplier collection plus outbound delivery consumes the contribution margin, reduce suppliers per bundle, renegotiate drop-off, increase lead time, or remove the bundle.

### 9.10 Month-12 operating scenarios

These are capacity cases, not revenue forecasts. Actual toman results come from the unit-economics sheet.

| Month-12 case | Completed orders/month | Approx. average orders/day | Operating shape | Inventory exposure | Supplier network | Discretionary cash ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| Downside | 25–30 | 1 | Founder-led; 6–8 base recipes; organic/concierge only; outsourced courier | Zero product stock; approximately ten orders of generic packaging | 2–3 active suppliers plus backups | 2M/month |
| Base | 50–60 | 2 | Founder-led with per-order or peak-hour packing help; manual controls; batched pickup/drop-off | Zero by default; at most 1–3 earned reusable SKUs from retained contribution | 3–4 active suppliers plus backups | 4M/month |
| Upside | 90–100 | 3–4 | Founder plus hourly/part-time support that passes the 1.5× coverage gate; route-based collection only when fully paid by orders | At most 3–5 earned SKUs, seven to fourteen demand days, within cash cap | 4–5 controlled suppliers plus backups | 7M/month, funded increasingly by contribution |

At an estimated 20 minutes of direct hub handling per order, these cases require roughly 8–10, 17–20, and 30–34 direct handling hours per month before receiving, procurement, content, stock count, and exceptions. No permanent role is assumed. Add hourly or part-time support only after the role gate is met; one occasion spike does not qualify.

### 9.11 Survivability test

Each month, run at least these stress cases:

- supplier costs rise 15% with retail prices unchanged for one week;
- courier cost rises 25%;
- 10% of orders require a service-recovery credit;
- a promotion discounts merchandise by 10%;
- same-day order density falls, increasing collection cost per order;
- one hero-bundle supplier is unavailable for seven days;
- order volume falls 50% during a digital disruption;
- the next monthly owner investment does not arrive;
- gateway settlement is delayed for seven days; and
- three refunds cluster in one week.

The business is resilient only if the downside case preserves cash, customer obligations, and core operations without emergency inventory liquidation or unpaid supplier balances.

## 10. Growth model

### 10.1 Funnel architecture

> **Instagram/search/referral discovery → Kadochi website trust → gift finder or collection → checkout → successful delivery → occasion/reminder/wishlist → repeat or referral**

Instagram follower count is not a primary business KPI. The meaningful Instagram metrics are qualified site visits, concierge conversations, account/reminder capture, and contribution-positive orders.

### 10.2 Instagram strategy

Organize content around customer decisions, not only product beauty. Production is founder-led, shot on a phone at the existing hub or supplier location, batched, and repurposed across formats. Use loaned samples, supplier assets, and consented UGC; do not fund a studio, agency retainer, or a separate purchased sample for every variant.

#### Content pillars

1. **For whom:** partner, friend, mother, father, colleague, child, teacher, host.
2. **For what moment:** birthday, anniversary, thank-you, apology, congratulations, get well, “just because.”
3. **For what budget:** entry, core, premium—framed respectfully.
4. **How soon:** ready today, tomorrow, or planned/personalized.
5. **Proof:** hub preparation, packing, supplier quality, delivery stories, real customer feedback.
6. **Guidance:** how to choose, what to write, common gifting mistakes, flower/cake care.
7. **Brand emotion:** stories about remembering and making someone feel seen.

#### Lean weekly cadence

- Two or three short-form videos focused on a customer situation, batch-produced from the same shoot.
- One or two carousel/static decision guides repurposed from the same topic.
- One real fulfillment/UGC proof item, with permission.
- Daily stories during operating days showing current readiness, questions, and social proof.
- One reusable long-form article or guide that also supports search and the magazine.

Every piece has one primary action: view a collection, use the gift finder, save an occasion, ask the concierge, or order. Do not include several competing calls to action.

### 10.3 Website and SEO strategy

The site should become the authoritative destination for purchase intent, not a passive catalog linked from Instagram.

Priority collection architecture:

- by recipient/relationship;
- by occasion;
- by budget band;
- by readiness;
- by desired feeling/style; and
- high-intent combinations such as “birthday gift for a partner, delivered today in Tehran.”

Each collection needs unique Persian guidance, a small number of fulfillable offers, transparent delivery expectations, and links to relevant magazine content. Do not create hundreds of thin pages or index out-of-stock assortments simply for SEO.

The editorial calendar should lead the public occasion calendar by 4–8 weeks, allowing pages, content, supplier capacity, and bundles to be ready before demand peaks.

### 10.4 Gift concierge

The concierge begins as a manual human service through approved channels. It should not become unstructured personal shopping.

Use a short decision record:

- relationship/recipient;
- occasion;
- budget band;
- delivery date;
- desired feeling/style;
- known preferences;
- recommended options;
- objection or decline reason; and
- outcome/order ID.

Service standard:

- Respond during operating hours within 15 minutes when channel conditions permit.
- Offer no more than three initial choices.
- Recommend only products whose availability and economics are known.
- Send the customer to a website checkout link rather than accepting unmanaged payment.
- Review tags weekly to identify missing bundles, unclear site copy, and frequent objections.

Automated/AI concierge work should wait until the manual taxonomy and fulfillment constraints are stable. Recommendation quality is not useful if it sends customers to unavailable products.

### 10.5 Owned retention

The most important retention asset is the occasion relationship.

After a successful order:

- invite the buyer to save the occasion and annual recurrence;
- ask for reminder consent and preferred timing;
- make repeat order possible from order history;
- let the user create or share a wishlist/profile;
- request a review after delivery, not before;
- offer a referral benefit that preserves contribution margin; and
- avoid sending generic promotions more often than useful occasion/value communication.

Recommended reminder sequence for a saved annual occasion:

- 21 days before: planning/inspiration.
- 7 days before: curated options across budget bands.
- 3 days before: ready/scheduled options and urgency.
- Day of occasion: same-day options only when genuinely available.

Users must be able to manage or stop reminders. Occasion and recipient data are relationship-sensitive and should be treated as trust data, not merely campaign fields.

### 10.6 Referral loop

Test two referral moments:

- Sender referral after confirmed successful delivery.
- Recipient referral or first-order invitation through discreet package material, with consent and without revealing private sender information.

Use fixed-value or value-added rewards rather than uncapped percentages. Credit becomes usable only after the referred order is successfully delivered and must have a clear validity policy that limits inflation exposure.

### 10.7 Paid acquisition gate

Do not scale paid traffic to compensate for weak organic conversion or unreliable fulfillment.

One discovery micro-test may run after the latest ten delivered orders are contribution-positive, tracking is complete, and cash remains Green; it follows the 0.75M cap in Section 9.3 and is not permission to scale. A second test requires the first to be first-order contribution-positive and at least 30 cumulative successful deliveries.

Repeatable paid acquisition or a second paid channel requires at least 50 successfully delivered orders, intent-specific landing pages, monthly variable contribution of at least 20%, and service KPIs within threshold for four consecutive weeks.

Use only one test cell at a time. Evaluate attributed first-order contribution, not clicks or impressions. Pause immediately when the test reaches its stop-loss; never fund a second cohort merely to average down a weak first result.

### 10.8 B2B pilot program

Offer three standardized packages—entry, core, premium—with controlled personalization choices.

Pilot terms:

- 5–30 gifts per initial campaign, or any larger quantity that is completely covered by cleared customer prepayment and confirmed supplier capacity.
- Tehran delivery only unless a parcel-safe item and carrier are explicitly approved.
- 100% prepayment preferred. If split, collect the higher of 80% of invoice value or 110% of all external commitments before procurement; collect the balance before dispatch.
- 24-hour quote validity for volatile contents.
- One consolidated contact and approved recipient-data template.
- No more than two customization rounds.
- Samples, branded notes, and any customization are client-funded before production; custom-manufactured Kadochi or client packaging is excluded.
- Delivery report after campaign.
- At least 20% variable contribution after coordination and recovery reserve.

Founder-led prospecting should contact approximately ten well-qualified organizations per week until one or two fully prepaid pilots are completed. The objective is to learn repeatable use cases and cash economics, not to build a separate sales organization.

## 11. Channel and business continuity

### 11.1 Principle

Kadochi cannot eliminate war, internet, SMS, banking, or courier disruption. It can avoid making one channel or system the only path to customer records and current orders.

### 11.2 Three operating modes

#### Green — normal connectivity

- Full website/PWA, Instagram, SMS/push, payment gateway, and normal supplier/courier channels.
- All approved promise classes available.
- Data exports and backups run on schedule.

#### Yellow — partial international-channel disruption

- Website/PWA and domestic-access status paths become primary.
- Instagram campaigns pause or shift to reusable owned content.
- Phone support, service SMS where available, and approved domestic messaging paths are highlighted.
- Same-day catalog narrows to hub-ready and highest-confidence suppliers.
- Staff perform twice-daily order/export reconciliation.

#### Red — severe communications, payment, or security disruption

- Public status states what is and is not operating.
- New orders are limited to products and zones the team can confirm manually.
- No campaign or promise is left active by default.
- A local/offline daily order ledger contains only the minimum operational customer data and is protected.
- Existing paid orders receive priority over new sales.
- If safe fulfillment cannot be guaranteed, orders are paused and customers receive a clear resolution when communication is possible.

### 11.3 Minimum continuity controls

- Daily export of open paid orders and essential fulfillment data.
- Weekly export of supplier contacts, active SKU/price list, and customer consent/reminder records.
- Two approved contact methods for critical suppliers and courier partners.
- A public status page or lightweight notice reachable independently of Instagram.
- A phone line with a recorded operating-status message when staffed response is unavailable.
- Printed or locally available hub checklists and supplier escalation contacts.
- Clear authority for one person to suspend same-day orders, campaigns, or all new checkout promises.
- Regular restore tests—not merely backups.

Continuity must comply with local law and protect personal data. It should not depend on unsafe circumvention instructions or expose recipient information through uncontrolled channels.

## 12. Organization and operating cadence

### 12.1 Roles, not titles

At low volume, one person may hold several roles. Accountability must still be explicit. The default year-one form is founder-operated, using suppliers, outsourced couriers, and hourly/per-order help. There is no fixed salary, new lease, agency, or permanent operations commitment until the role gate in Section 9.4 is passed.

| Role | Accountable for |
|---|---|
| General manager/founder | Strategy, cash, supplier/commercial exceptions, B2B sales, and phase-gate decisions |
| Merchandising and growth | Hero bundles, price bands, content, site collections, campaigns, experiments, and demand insights |
| Operations and procurement | Supplier confirmations, stock, hub flow, QC, dispatch, service KPIs, and root-cause correction |
| Customer experience | Concierge, order exceptions, recipient/sender communication, reviews, refunds, and retention consent |
| Finance/analytics | Order economics, supplier settlement, KPI definitions, attribution, cash forecast, and stress tests |
| Product/technology | Tracking reliability, promise presentation, checkout support, reminders, operational tools, and security/continuity backlog |

### 12.2 Daily cadence

- Opening: supplier price/availability confirmation and service-capacity check.
- Morning: open order review, exceptions, and pickup/dispatch plan.
- Midday: same-day cutoff and capacity decision.
- Before each dispatch window: order-by-order readiness and QC check.
- Closing: delivered/failed reconciliation, stock adjustments, refunds/exceptions, and next-day confirmation.

### 12.3 Weekly cadence

**Monday — commercial and demand review**  
Traffic, conversion, orders, content, concierge tags, upcoming occasions, and campaign plan.

**Wednesday — catalog and supply review**  
SKU scores, supplier scores, prices, stock age, bundle readiness, and delisting decisions.

**Saturday or end of operating week — economics and service review**  
Contribution by order/bundle/channel, OTIF, cancellations, refunds, customer complaints, cash, and phase gates.

The meeting output is a short decision log: continue, change, stop, owner, and due date. Reporting without decisions is not a cadence.

### 12.4 Monthly review

- Scenario versus actual orders and contribution.
- Fixed costs covered by monthly contribution.
- Cohort repeat and acquisition economics.
- Supplier and courier concentration risk.
- Inventory aging and write-off exposure.
- B2B pipeline, deposits, margin, and delivery performance.
- Product backlog tied to measured commercial bottlenecks.
- Decision on whether any growth gate has been earned.

## 13. Twelve-month execution roadmap

The phases below are evidence- and cash-gated. Calendar time does not unlock spending.

### Phase 0 — Days 0–7: stop leakage and establish cash truth

#### Deliverables

- Ring-fence the protected floor (minimum 12M) and list every current liability.
- Build a rolling 13-week cash forecast with opening cash, expected owner funding, customer receipts, order liabilities, unavoidable costs, and weekly closing unrestricted cash.
- Reconstruct full economics for the latest 20 orders, or all orders if fewer exist.
- Hide every product without verified current cost, availability, lead time, and contribution.
- Select 6–8 launch base recipes and 2–3 core suppliers plus critical backups.
- Build the modular packaging prototype within the 1M cap.
- Freeze all software work except payment, checkout, fulfillment, security, cost-tracking, or consent defects.
- Create the daily order-control/QC sheet and assign the person who can suspend offers.

#### Exit gate

- Unrestricted cash and committed obligations are known.
- The protected floor is intact.
- At least six offers can be customer-funded and priced to the 20% contribution floor.
- No promoted offer requires advance finished-goods inventory.

### Phase 1 — Days 8–30: run the customer-funded model

#### Deliverables

- Launch 6–8 fully confirmed recipes; add variants through size/message/add-ons rather than new stock.
- Require confirmed customer payment before procurement; use only the revolving order float if gateway settlement has not reached the bank, and reconfirm suppliers daily.
- Record all product, packaging, collection, delivery, fee, labor, recovery, and acquisition costs per order.
- Publish accurate next-day/scheduled promises; reserve same-day for truly confirmed single-path orders.
- Run founder-led Instagram content and the structured manual concierge.
- Interview every willing purchaser and all high-intent non-buyers.
- Ask successful buyers to save an occasion/reminder.
- Spend zero on paid acquisition.

#### Exit gate

- Latest ten delivered orders are positive in aggregate contribution and no individual loss lacks a documented acquisition/recovery reason.
- OTIF is at least 95% or every failure has a corrected root cause.
- No unresolved customer liability exists.
- Unrestricted cash remains at or above the protected floor.

### Phase 2 — Days 31–60: remove weak offers and test one growth lever

#### Deliverables

- Remove or redesign the lowest-performing recipes and suppliers.
- Activate reminder, recipient-safe referral, and repeat-order follow-up.
- Run at most one paid test up to 0.75M if the Phase 1 gate passed.
- Attempt one small fully prepaid B2B pilot whose deposit covers every commitment.
- Introduce supplier scorecards and weekly cash/economics review.
- Target 20–30 cumulative successfully delivered orders with complete cost data.

#### Exit gate

- Blended product gross margin is at least 38%.
- Variable contribution margin is at least 20%.
- OTIF is at least 95% and supplier-caused cancellation below 2%.
- The paid test, if run, stays within its stop-loss.
- Protected cash remains intact.

### Phase 3 — Days 61–90: decide whether the model has earned continuation

#### Deliverables

- Retain only recipes with reliable fulfillment and positive contribution.
- Run a second paid test only if the first was first-order contribution-positive.
- Complete one or two fully prepaid B2B pilots; accept no credit terms.
- Improve the highest-intent collection pages using observed questions.
- Target 30–50 cumulative successfully delivered orders, at least 25% reminder opt-in, and complete weekly reporting.
- Continue with zero finished-goods inventory.

#### Day-90 decision

- **Under 30 deliveries or contribution below 20%:** return to a maintenance offer of flowers plus a few on-demand add-ons; freeze growth spending.
- **30–49 deliveries with positive economics:** continue the lean model; do not add inventory, staff, or fixed costs.
- **At least 50 deliveries plus all reliability, margin, and cash gates:** release the next controlled growth tranche, still without product inventory.

### Phase 4 — Months 4–6: retain winners and build repeat demand

#### Deliverables

- Graduate, redesign, or delist every tested recipe.
- Improve SEO/collection pages and gift-finder paths using existing infrastructure.
- Measure reminder, referral, repeat, and first-order acquisition cohorts.
- Negotiate consignment or weekly settlement from proven volume.
- Consider at most 1–3 earned Tier 3 SKUs only after the inventory gate passes and only from retained contribution.
- Continue standardized, fully prepaid B2B work only where contribution is at least 20%.

#### Exit gate

- Service and contribution thresholds hold for eight consecutive weeks.
- Reminder opt-in among eligible purchasers reaches at least 25%.
- 90-day repeat rate for mature B2C cohorts reaches at least 15% or shows a credible upward path.
- Paid cohorts, if used, meet the first-order contribution rule.
- Monthly contribution covers at least half of unavoidable monthly cash cost.
- Unrestricted cash remains at or above the protected floor without relying on the next injection.

### Phase 5 — Months 7–12: scale only from earned cash

#### Deliverables

- Concentrate content and availability on the best recipes and occasions.
- Add hourly/per-order operations capacity only after the 1.5× role gate.
- Automate only repeated bottlenecks using existing platform capability or very small reversible changes.
- Introduce repeat/reorder and stronger occasion-to-product paths where they can be implemented without material new development spend.
- Expand B2B only through standardized, fully cash-covered packages.
- Research national parcel demand and costs, but defer a live expansion test unless three consecutive months of positive operating cash flow fund it without touching the reserve.

#### Year-one success condition

The capital-constrained base case is successful if Kadochi reaches approximately 50–60 successfully delivered monthly orders while maintaining:

- unrestricted cash at or above the protected floor plus full customer-obligation coverage;
- at least 20% variable contribution margin;
- at least 38% blended product gross margin;
- at least 95% OTIF;
- less than 2% supplier-caused cancellations;
- zero speculative inventory and no more than 14 days for any earned stocked SKU;
- no fixed-cost hire, lease, agency, or custom-packaging commitment that fails its cash gate; and
- growing repeat/reminder contribution.

Reaching 90–100 orders with weak margins or new cash dependence is not success. Reaching 50–60 with protected cash, reliable fulfillment, and an increasing share of contribution-funded growth is.

## 14. Three-year direction

### Year 1 — prove the Tehran model

- Build a controlled gift system around the existing product.
- Establish customer-funded supplier, zero-cost hub, and courier routines.
- Prove accessible-premium bundles across common occasions.
- Convert Instagram demand into owned accounts, reminders, and repeat cohorts.
- Validate prepaid B2B as a complementary use case.
- Preserve the cash floor and avoid speculative inventory, custom packaging, new leases, and permanent hires.

### Year 2 — deepen density and selective reach

Expansion order:

1. Increase order density and supplier economics in Tehran.
2. Add selected nationwide parcel delivery for durable products from the hub.
3. Test additional local delivery geography only where demand, supplier quality, and an accountable operating partner exist.
4. Add recipient preference and repeat-gifting intelligence.
5. Build supplier tools only for active partners with meaningful volume.

National parcel expansion requires three consecutive months of positive operating cash flow, Tehran contribution margin at or above 20%, OTIF at or above 95%, and enough unrestricted cash to fund a capped parcel test without touching the 12M floor or expected owner investment.

### Year 3 — become the occasion relationship layer

Kadochi's moat should increasingly come from its permissioned occasion and gifting graph:

- who the customer gives to;
- which occasions matter;
- budget and style preferences;
- what was sent previously;
- recipient feedback and wishlist signals; and
- which offers can be fulfilled in each place and timeframe.

Use this to improve recommendations, repeat ordering, B2B programs, and supplier planning. Consider a controlled partner platform only when Kadochi can enforce availability, price, quality, delivery, data, and service-recovery standards. A marketplace is a possible later operating form, not the current strategy.

## 15. Management dashboard

### 15.1 North-star metric

**Successfully delivered gift orders:** Orders delivered inside the promised window, with ordered/approved contents, that do not produce a quality refund or material complaint within 48 hours.

This metric protects the emotional outcome while remaining operationally measurable.

### 15.2 Core scorecard

| Metric | Definition | Year-one target/guardrail | Cadence | Triggered action |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| Unrestricted cash | Bank cash minus paid-undelivered and other committed liabilities | ≥protected floor (minimum 12M) | Daily/weekly | Apply Amber/Red/Critical stop-loss |
| Customer-obligation coverage | Liquid cash available for open orders ÷ committed open-order obligations | ≥1.0 at all times | Daily | Pause advance-requiring orders; protect refunds/fulfillment |
| 13-week cash outlook | Lowest forecast weekly unrestricted-cash balance | Never below protected floor | Weekly | Freeze unreleased spend; renegotiate timing/cost |
| Monthly net burn | Operating cash outflow minus retained order contribution, excluding restricted order flows | Within released budget and declining | Monthly | Reset catalog/spend; do not assume next injection |
| Fixed-cost coverage | Monthly contribution ÷ unavoidable monthly cash cost | ≥0.5 by month 6; ≥1.0 by month 12 | Monthly | Reduce costs or raise absolute contribution/order |
| Fully customer-funded orders | Orders with cleared customer funds covering external commitments ÷ orders | 100% target; ≥95% hard guardrail | Weekly | Remove/retime offer or change supplier terms |
| Successfully delivered orders | North-star orders per month | Scenario: 25–30 / 50–60 / 90–100 | Weekly/monthly | Capacity or demand action; never viewed without cash/margin |
| Qualified website conversion | Paid orders ÷ qualified purchase-intent sessions | Establish baseline; aim ≥1.5% by month 3 and ≥2.0% by month 6 | Weekly | Review page, trust, price, availability, checkout |
| AOV | Net order revenue ÷ paid orders | Track by band/channel; grow through relevant attachment | Weekly | Adjust bundles/add-ons, not arbitrary price increases |
| Product gross margin | Product gross profit ÷ merchandise revenue | ≥38% blended; ≥35% new SKU | Weekly | Reprice, renegotiate, redesign, or delist |
| Variable contribution margin | Variable contribution ÷ net order revenue | ≥20% B2C/B2B; ≥25% multi-supplier | Weekly/monthly | Pause promotions/paid scale; fix cost driver |
| Paid CAC | Attributed acquisition spend ÷ new paid customers | First-order contribution non-negative before scaling | Per cohort | Pause or narrow channel |
| 90-day repeat rate | Mature buyers with another order inside 90 days | ≥15% by phase-3 exit | Monthly cohort | Improve reminders, repeat path, offer relevance |
| 180-day repeat rate | Mature buyers with another order inside 180 days | ≥25% by year end | Monthly cohort | Reassess retention and occasion capture |
| Reminder opt-in | Eligible purchasers/users saving permissioned reminder | ≥25% | Weekly | Improve timing and value explanation |
| Reminder conversion | Reminder recipients ordering within attributed window | Initial target ≥8%; refine after baseline | Per campaign | Improve timing, relevance, readiness |
| Hero-bundle share | Orders containing a hero bundle ÷ all orders | ≥50% by day 90 | Weekly | Simplify collections or strengthen bundles |
| Add-on attachment | Orders with approved add-on ÷ eligible orders | ≥35% | Weekly | Improve relevance/presentation; avoid clutter |
| OTIF | Orders delivered on time with all approved contents | ≥95% | Daily/weekly | Stop affected promotion; root-cause correction |
| Supplier cancellation | Supplier-caused cancellations ÷ accepted orders | <2% | Weekly | Probation, backup routing, delisting |
| Material substitution | Orders with material substitution ÷ delivered orders | <5%; required approvals 100% | Weekly | Fix availability/content/supplier |
| Damage/quality refund | Affected delivered orders ÷ delivered orders | <3% | Weekly | Packaging, supplier, or courier correction |
| Inventory days | On-hand COGS ÷ average daily sold COGS | Zero first 90 days; ≤14 days after earned gate | Weekly | Freeze purchasing; return/bundle/delist |
| Supplier score | Weighted supplier score | ≥80 active; ≥90 preferred | Monthly | Corrective action or suspension |
| B2B cash cycle | Days from procurement cash out to customer cash collection | Customer cash first; no post-delivery receivable | Per project | Tighten terms or decline work |

### 15.3 Dashboard rules

- Use one agreed formula per metric; do not change historical definitions silently.
- Separate B2C, B2B, organic, paid, and major occasion campaigns.
- Show both percentage and denominator; 0% cancellations across three orders is not proof.
- Attach a root-cause code to cancellation, refund, lateness, and substitution.
- Review cohorts by first-order month, not only aggregate repeat rate.
- Treat missing cost data as an operational defect, not zero cost.

## 17. Risk register and scenario playbooks

| Scenario | Early signal | Immediate action | Customer promise | Recovery gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supplier failure | Missed confirmations, stale stock, QC decline | Route to approved backup; stop promotion; contact affected buyers before slot | No silent substitution | Two-week corrective proof and score ≥80 |
| Inflation/price shock | Cost movement >5%, rejected price locks | Refresh prices, shorten quote validity, freeze affected promotions | Honor accepted order where practicable; explain exceptions | Margin restored without misleading discounting |
| Internet/SMS disruption | Channel delivery failures, traffic collapse | Move to Yellow/Red mode; narrow catalog; publish status; reconcile exports | Accept only confirmable orders | Stable communication and order reconciliation |
| Courier shortage/cost spike | Low acceptance, ETA expansion, surge cost | Narrow zones/windows, prioritize paid orders, charge transparent rate | Do not advertise unavailable same-day service | Two providers/capacity restored; economics retested |
| Payment disruption | Gateway errors, reconciliation mismatch | Pause new paid checkout if status is uncertain; retain operation ID; reconcile before retry | No duplicate charge/order | Gateway and order reconciliation tested |
| Cash exhaustion | Unrestricted cash approaches 12M, negative 13-week low, contribution shortfall | Apply cash stop-loss; freeze ads, samples, subsidies, product work, and advance purchases | Protect existing paid orders before new growth | Cash ≥12M plus obligations and positive 30-day contribution |
| Missed owner investment | Expected monthly funding is delayed or absent | Remove it from forecast immediately; release no spend against it | Continue only customer-funded offers | Plan works for 13 weeks without assumed injection |
| Delayed settlement/refund cluster | Gateway cash late or several refunds in one week | Use only revolving/recovery reserve; stop advance-requiring orders if coverage tightens | Resolve paid customers first | Settlement reconciled and reserves restored |
| War/security closure | Unsafe hub/supplier/courier operation | Suspend new orders; protect staff; account for open paid orders | Safety overrides delivery; proactive resolution when possible | Explicit management restart decision |
| Occasion demand spike | Capacity >80%, supplier rejection rise | Cap bundles/slots, simplify catalog, add planned shifts, stop low-priority campaigns | Sell fewer slots honestly | Capacity and OTIF remain within thresholds |
| Food/quality incident | Allergen complaint, spoilage, repeated QC failure | Quarantine batch/SKU, stop supplier, contact affected customers, preserve records | Refund/replacement and safety-first communication | Documented supplier and handling correction |
| Customer-data exposure | Lost export/device, uncontrolled message sharing | Restrict access, preserve evidence, follow applicable notification/legal process | Honest, minimal, privacy-protective response | Security review and controls completed |

### Tabletop schedule

Run one 45-minute scenario every month. Participants should identify:

- decision owner;
- information needed;
- orders/customers affected;
- safe operating status;
- communication message;
- cash/refund effect;
- recovery gate; and
- one control to improve before the next exercise.

Rotate through supplier failure, internet/SMS disruption, courier shortage, payment uncertainty, security closure, and occasion surge during the first six months.

## 18. Validation plan

### 18.1 First 90-day hypotheses

| Hypothesis | Test | Success criterion | Decision if false |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers prefer complete decisions to a broad catalog | Compare hero-bundle collections with equivalent individual-product browsing | Bundle share ≥50% and conversion/contribution no worse than individual paths | Redesign bundles/merchandising; do not expand SKU count |
| Three price bands reduce price anxiety | Show entry/core/premium for matched intents; collect concierge objections | Improved selection rate and fewer “too expensive/no clear choice” objections | Adjust KGI ranges, contents, or language |
| Hub consolidation improves reliability/economics | Compare consolidated versus fragmented orders | Lower failure rate and collection/delivery cost per successful order | Reduce supplier count per bundle or change flow |
| Occasion capture creates permissioned retention | Ask eligible successful buyers to save an occasion | ≥25% opt-in | Change timing/value explanation and consent flow |
| Instagram can produce website orders, not only DMs | Use tagged content and dedicated collection links | Attributable qualified visits and contribution-positive orders | Rework CTA/page match; preserve concierge as bridge |
| Customer-funded sourcing can support the offer | Track cleared customer cash against every external commitment | ≥95% fully customer-funded orders; 100% target | Narrow to single-supplier flowers/on-demand add-ons and renegotiate terms |
| A standardized B2B offer is viable | Complete 1–2 fully prepaid pilots | ≥20% contribution, all commitments cash-covered, acceptable OTIF, repeat interest | Keep B2B opportunistic or stop the use case |
| Earned micro-inventory improves economics | Only after day 90, stock 1–3 proven reusable components from retained contribution | ≤14 inventory days, ≥35% attachment, payback within 30 days, no reserve use | Return immediately to reservation/post-order sourcing |

### 18.2 Customer research

Use short interviews tied to observed behavior:

- purchasers: why Kadochi, what nearly stopped them, price perception, trust, and delivery result;
- concierge non-buyers: missing product, price, timing, trust, or checkout issue;
- recipients: presentation, delivery, privacy, and whether the gift felt intentional;
- B2B buyers: internal coordination removed, reporting needs, customization, and payment terms.

Avoid asking “Would you use this?” Ask about the last real gifting occasion, alternatives considered, money/time spent, and what went wrong.

### 18.3 Phase-gate authority

Only the general manager/founder can approve expansion beyond a failed gate, and the exception must state:

- which threshold failed;
- why expansion is still justified;
- maximum cash and customer exposure;
- test duration; and
- automatic stop condition.

This prevents temporary excitement around followers, a campaign, or a single corporate lead from overriding the operating model.

## 19. What Kadochi will not do

During the first 12 months, Kadochi will not:

- chase catalog size as a success metric;
- list products without confirmed economics and fulfillment responsibility;
- promise same-day delivery for a basket with an unconfirmed component;
- buy finished-goods inventory during the first 90 days or use the protected floor/owner injection for speculative stock;
- spend in an attempt to “recover” the sunk 100M already invested;
- commission custom-manufactured branded packaging before its cash/payback gate;
- purchase supplier minimum quantities for unproven demand;
- assume the next 10–15M monthly investment before it arrives;
- subsidize structurally negative orders with owner cash;
- sign a new lease, agency retainer, or permanent hire before the applicable cash gate;
- compete as the lowest-price gift store;
- depend on Instagram as the only customer record or ordering path;
- make silent material substitutions;
- scale paid acquisition while first-order contribution is negative;
- accept large credit-heavy or highly customized B2B work;
- open marketplace seller onboarding;
- expand local-delivery geography before Tehran gates are sustained;
- automate an undefined or unstable manual process; or
- treat a late emotional gift like an ordinary parcel failure.

These boundaries are strategic assets. They protect cash, trust, and management attention.

## 20. Final recommendation

Kadochi's opportunity is not to out-stock a marketplace or out-network every flower-delivery business. It is to become the service customers trust with the full emotional job of gifting.

The winning model is:

- **Flowers for immediate relevance and emotional impact.**
- **Customer-funded curated local gifts and bundles for variety and margin.**
- **A zero-incremental-cost hub for one finished experience.**
- **Modular off-the-shelf packaging for tasteful presentation without capital lock-up.**
- **Supplier discipline for reliable promises.**
- **Occasion memory and guided choice for repeat demand.**
- **Instagram for discovery, owned channels for resilience.**
- **Contribution margin and OTIF gates for responsible growth.**

If Kadochi consistently helps a customer remember, choose, and deliver successfully, it can earn repeat behavior without constant discounting, inventory, or an enormous catalog. The near-term model is **customer-funded commerce first**: sell a confirmed solution, collect cash, procure, consolidate, deliver, and retain the contribution. That is the most capital-efficient and defensible path through Iran's current market conditions.
